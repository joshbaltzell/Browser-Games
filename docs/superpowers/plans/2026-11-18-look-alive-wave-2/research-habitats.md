## PART A: Alkaline hot spring outflow mat (Octopus / Mushroom Spring, Yellowstone)

### 1. Temperature zones and who lives there
- **~82-88 C, upper outflow: pink streamers of *Thermocrinis ruber*** (Aquificae, chemolithotroph, no photosynthesis). Rods ~1-3 x 0.4-0.5 um; single motile rods, aggregates or long filaments forming streamers. Growth 44-89 C, optimum 80 C. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC106467/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermocrinis_ruber (size from secondary source: UNSURE as primary). Note: original strain DSM 12173 was later identified as *T. albus*; name *T. ruber* still valid. https://www.dsmz.de/collection/catalogue/details/culture/DSM-12173
- **~73 C: the photosynthesis line.** Below about 72-73 C the mat turns green. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364356
- **~70 C: *Thermus aquaticus*** (heterotroph, the Taq enzyme source), first isolated from Mushroom Spring by Brock and Freeze in 1969; rods 0.5-0.8 um wide. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermus_aquaticus . Whether it is a dominant mat member: UNSURE (it is a widespread minor heterotroph).
- **~72 down to ~50 C: unicellular cyanobacteria "Synechococcus A/B lineage", now genus *Thermostichus*** (Komárek & Strunecký 2020; type species *T. vulcanus*; also proposed as *Leptococcus*). Short cylindrical cells. https://lpsn.dsmz.de/genus/thermostichus ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9600594
  - Ecotypes hottest to coolest: A'', A', A, B', B. Isolates: OS-A ~58-70 C (no phosphonate use, cannot grow heterotrophically); OS-B' ~53-60 C. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.03.25.485844v1.full-text
  - Older literature calls them *Synechococcus lividus*. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/5206182
  - Cell size: UNSURE in this pass; my recollection is ~1-2 um wide, a few um long (verify).
- **~72-50 C, under and among the cyanobacteria: filamentous anoxygenic phototrophs (Chloroflexota).**
  - *Roseiflexus castenholzii* dominates the orange undermat in non-sulfidic springs like these; *Chloroflexus* dominates sulfidic springs. https://www.nature.com/articles/ismej2007117 ; https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/the-dark-side-of-the-mushroom-spring-microbial-mat-life-in-the-sh-2/
  - *Chloroflexus aurantiacus*: filaments 0.6-0.7 um wide, indefinite length. https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF00446302 (species description; via search snippet)
  - *Roseiflexus* filament width: UNSURE (about 0.8-1 um from memory).
  - Also "Ca. Roseilinea gracile" in the green layer. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2017.00943/pdf
- **Below ~58 C: *Fischerella* (= *Mastigocladus laminosus*)**, branching heterocyst-forming filaments; field limit ~58 C (lab cultures from Iceland to ~62-64 C). At White Creek its streamers sit below ~55 C. https://academic.oup.com/femsec/article/63/1/23/490717 ; https://openpolar.no/Record/crwiley:10.1111%2Fj.1529-8817.1969.tb02626.x
- **Below ~54 C: *Oscillatoria terebriformis*** (sulfidic Hunter's Hot Springs, Oregon, not Yellowstone): mat surface only below 54 C. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC204072 . *Geitlerinema* in this mat: UNSURE.
- **Below ~40 C: grazers move in** (see 5).

### 2. Real upper limits
- Photosynthesis: ~72-73 C; one Yellowstone *Synechococcus* biofilm measured upper limit 71.7 C; carbon fixation peaked at 65 C. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC106575 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364356
- Eukaryotes: classic limit ~60 C (Tansey & Brock 1972, PNAS). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4506763 . Eukaryotic algae (*Cyanidium*) top out ~55-60 C. NEW (2026, Cell): amoeba *Incendiamoeba cascadensis* from Lassen Volcanic NP divides at 63 C, moves at 64 C, encysts at 70 C. https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/evolution/newfound-fire-amoeba-from-the-cascades-sets-record-for-the-hottest-temperature-complex-life-can-survive-at

### 3. Heat adaptations (card material)
- **Heat shock chaperones** (GroEL/DnaK in bacteria, thermosome in archaea) refold heat-damaged proteins. Textbook.
- **Reverse gyrase**: adds positive supercoils to DNA; the only protein found in all hyperthermophiles (>80 C) and absent from mesophiles; carried by Aquificales. Knockouts can still grow poorly, so "required" is debated. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2686386 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6878951
- **Ether lipids / tetraether monolayer** (archaea): chemically stable bonds plus a single spanning layer cut leakage; more cyclopentane rings at higher temperature (*Sulfolobus* 3.4 to 4.8 rings from 65 to 82 C). Not strictly required. https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s42003-021-02178-y ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426160
- **Ester lipids, tuned** (bacteria like *Thermus*, *Synechococcus*): longer, more saturated, branched fatty acids keep the membrane from getting too fluid. Textbook; no specific source this pass.
- **Thermostable proteins**: more salt bridges, tighter hydrophobic cores, fewer heat-labile residues. Textbook.
- **Heat-stable photosystem**: *Thermostichus* PSII and phycobilisome got more heat-stable as lineages moved hotter, at a cost of cool-temperature performance (a real trade-off). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364356
- **Polyamines**: *Thermus thermophilus* makes long/branched polyamines at 80 C that protect DNA, ribosomes and tRNA; mutants lacking them fail above 75 C. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9654539
- **DNA repair**: hyperthermophiles face constant depurination and strand breaks; the 2026 fire amoeba upregulates DNA repair and proteostasis. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2686386 ; https://www.the-scientist.com/a-fire-amoeba-takes-first-place-for-eukaryotic-heat-tolerance-75025

### 4. Motility and behaviour
- *Thermostichus* OS-A and OS-B' show phototaxis and photokinesis, powered by type IV pili (pilB mutant non-motile). OS-B' answers UVA/blue/green/red; OS-A mainly red/green, sometimes negative phototaxis. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11804611
- *Chloroflexus* glides without steering toward light; paired with *Thermostichus* the two move farther together and form ordered arrays (Bunbury et al. 2025, PNAS). https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/14494/files/bunbury-et-al-2025-cyanobacteria-and-chloroflexota-cooperate-to-structure-light-responsive-biofilms.pdf . Gliding speed: 0.01-0.04 um/s (1974 agar) vs ~1-3 um/s (2023 methods); conflicting.
- Vertical migration: *O. terebriformis* migrates daily (diel) (Hunter's HS). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC204072 . For *Thermostichus*, diel shifts in vertical distribution are reported but not directly tracked per species (partly UNSURE).
- Diel chemistry swing: at 1 mm depth, 55 C site, O2 ~900 uM (strongly supersaturated) and pH 9.4 after noon; anoxic, pH 7.2 before sunrise. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC241501 . Night: glycogen fermented to acetate/propionate; N2 fixation at night. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3446795 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/ismej2007117
- **Thermotaxis: UNSURE / not documented** in these organisms. One abstract says *Synechococcus* motility depends on temperature, but no directed movement along a heat gradient was found.

### 5. Guard analogues
- **Brine fly larvae (*Paracoenia turbida*, Ephydridae)**: wipe out all mat below their egg sites; eggs do not hatch and first instars die above 40 C. Most of the mat is too hot or under flowing water. https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF00016450.pdf ; https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65dec9ac0f3e94b9e5d15e92
- **Amoebae** (*Echinamoeba*, *Naegleria*): optima up to ~55 C; ciliates common in NZ springs. https://www.nature.com/articles/ismej2012157.pdf . Exact limits for ciliates/nematodes in these mats: UNSURE.
- **Phage**: cyanophage virome from 60 C Octopus Spring mat; all three dominant phototrophs carry CRISPR with spacers matching mat phages, so it is an active arms race. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2612747 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5017753 . Viruses continue above 80 C (archaeal viruses in acidic springs) but the exact cutoff here is UNSURE.

### 6. The counter-intuitive rule (verified)
- Grazing flies can only live where it is cool (<40 C larvae), so the cool, comfortable margin is the eaten zone, and hot flowing water protects the mat. Diverted, cooled mat gets eaten. https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65dec9ac0f3e94b9e5d15e92 . Same pattern for all eukaryote grazers above ~60 C (none).

### 7. Size and what you see
- Living photosynthetic layer only ~0.5-1.1 mm (green); orange undermat below. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/5314849 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4400912
- Mat grows ~50 um/day; ~2 cm of mat can decompose in a year; Sulfide Spring mats 2-4 cm deep. Octopus total thickness: UNSURE (cm scale). https://www.osti.gov/biblio/5206182
- Visible: pink streamers (*Thermocrinis*) up top, green/yellow-green surface, orange undermat, *Fischerella* streamers at cooler edge, brine flies and larvae at the cool rim.

## PART B

### (a) Hypersaline saltern / salt flat
- Natives: ***Haloquadratum walsbyi*** (archaea; flat square cells 0.1-0.2 um thick, sheets to ~40 um; up to 80-90% of cells, 10^8/mL) https://www.doi.org/10.1186/s12864-017-3892-2 ; ***Halobacterium salinarum*** (rods, size UNSURE); ***Salinibacter ruber*** (red bacterium, needs >=1.7 M NaCl) https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2013.00315/full ; ***Dunaliella salina*** (green alga, size UNSURE ~10-20 um); ***Artemia*** brine shrimp (adults ~1 cm, UNSURE exact).
- Adaptations: salt-in KCl with acidic proteome (Salinibacter median pI 5.92, *Halobacterium* 5.03) https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2013.00315/full ; gas vesicles keep *Haloquadratum* at the surface https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/1471-2164-7-169 ; glycerol as compatible solute in *Dunaliella* (textbook; Oren 2005 Saline Systems review, not opened).
- Guard: haloviruses, 4x10^8 to 2x10^9 per mL, highest virus counts known in water; few grazers at saturation (but some found at 31% salt). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298169
- Supply: scarce/steady organic input; wet-dry cycles. Artemia salinity ceiling and wet-dry details: UNSURE.
- Largest in a dish: *Artemia*.

### (b) Deep ocean water and sediment
- Natives: ***Photobacterium profundum*** SS9 (best at 28 MPa, 15 C; grows 0.1-90 MPa) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3669370 ; ***Moritella yayanosii*** (Challenger Deep, 10,898 m; obligate, optimum 80 MPa per genome record) https://www.pubmed.gov/34319226/ ; ***Shewanella benthica*** (UNSURE, not checked); ***Pelagibacter ubique*** (SAR11; ~0.2 x 0.4 um, ~0.01 um^3; <0.5 divisions/day) https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=115570 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10308942 ; ***Nitrosopumilus maritimus*** (Thaumarchaeota/Nitrososphaerota; size UNSURE).
- Adaptations: more unsaturated fatty acids keep membrane fluid under pressure (exceptions exist) https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2022.1058381/full ; TMAO piezolyte https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7997507 ; slow growth / dormancy.
- Slow life: subseafloor biomass turnover hundreds to thousands of years (method-dependent; some new estimates years to decades). https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10905 ; https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nrmicro2939
- Guard: viruses, "Kill the Winner" (Thingstad 2000): viruses hit the most abundant, fastest grower, so being too successful is punished. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_the_Winner_hypothesis . Field support is mixed.
- Supply: pulsed. Much of the yearly food reaches the abyss in pulses of a few days; oxygen use doubles at once. https://www.nature.com/articles/nature01799
- Largest in a dish: foraminifera or small macrofauna (polychaete worms). UNSURE size.

### (c) Forest soil with fungal network
- Natives: ***Streptomyces*** (branching hyphae, spores, geosmin; sizes UNSURE ~1 um wide); ***Bacillus*** (endospores; textbook); ***Pseudomonas***; ***Rhizobium*** / ***Frankia*** (root nodules); mycorrhizal fungi (hyphae ~2-10 um wide, textbook).
- Adaptations: endospores; antibiotic production; riding fungal hyphae ("fungal highway") and carrying phage along. https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/938641 ; https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00248-017-1137-1.pdf
- Network signals: claims that tree-to-tree mycorrhizal networks share carbon and warnings are weakly supported (Karst et al. 2023), still debated. https://www.peeref.com/works/27827434 . Hyphal electrical signalling: UNSURE.
- Scale: *Armillaria ostoyae* in Malheur NF, ~900-965 ha, one genet. https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/humongous-fungus-armillaria-ostoyae/
- Guards: protists, nematodes (~190k-270k /m^2 in top 6 cm), springtails (~90k-118k /m^2; up to 2M /m^2 in tundra). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2903492/table/pone-0011567-t001
- Supply: patchy and pulsed (root exudates, leaf fall, rain). Textbook.
- Largest in a dish: springtail or mite.

## Size ladder (about 10x per step, roughly)
- Bacterium: ~1-2 um (*Pelagibacter* 0.2-0.4 um, above).
- Ciliate/amoeba: ~20-300 um (textbook; UNSURE on exact range).
- Rotifer or tardigrade: tardigrades mostly 0.3-0.5 mm (0.05-1.7 mm). https://soilbugs.massey.ac.nz/tardigrada.php . Rotifer: UNSURE.
- Nematode: soil species mostly up to ~2 mm (0.3-10 mm). https://www.sare.org/publications/farming-with-soil-life/mesofauna-non-arthropods/
- Springtail: 0.25-5 mm. https://sare.org/publications/farming-with-soil-life/mesofauna-arthropods . Note: tardigrade, nematode and springtail sizes overlap, so the ladder is closer to bacterium x10 ciliate x10 tardigrade x3-5 nematode/springtail.
